"SURPLUS", in BEA data, includes all capital returns: 
including profits, rents, interest 
[shared capital]. This definition comes oddly 
close to Marx's definition of "surplus value".

According to Karl Marx, surplus value is the difference 
between the total value a worker produces and the 
actual wages they are paid. So, here too, with data from the BEA, 
surplus is counted as: 
1. The 'rents' paid to the owner of the land used for the factory;
2. the 'interest' paid on loans that helped capitalize production;
3.  the net 'profits' earned by the sale of the product after all 
other inputs, especially compensation, have been subtracted.
