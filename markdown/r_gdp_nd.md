State-Run Enterprise: 

North Dakota operates the nation's only 
state-owned bank, the Bank of North Dakota, 
which partners with local financial institutions 
to stimulate economic development and keep 
capital within the state.

Dominant Industries: 

The economy is heavily dependent on energy 
(the state is a top-tier U.S. oil producer) 
and production agriculture (generating 
over $41 billion in annual revenue). Recent 
state investments, such as large soybean 
processing plants, aim to insulate farmers 
from volatile export markets.


Fiscal Stability: The state’s fiscal policy 
relies heavily on charges, sales taxes, and 
severance taxes on resource extraction, 
resulting in a robust tax base that historically 
mitigates recession risks.

Education:

North Dakota funds public education through 
a combination of state revenue and local 
property taxes, with the state covering 
the majority of the cost.

The funding breakdown operates as follows:
State vs. Local Share: The state covers roughly 
70% to 75% of educational costs, while local 
property taxes account for about 23%.The 
Funding Formula: The state determines an 
adequate amount of funding per student. 
To meet this, school districts are required 
to levy a local property tax of 60 mills, 
and the state covers the remaining balance 
to ensure equity across districts.

Capital Outlay: While general operational costs 
are split, building projects, capital outlays, 
and local debt services are funded almost 
exclusively by local property taxes.

    
Healthcare:

North Dakota typically ranks in the top tier
of US states for overall health care and 
public health metrics, performing exceptionally 
well in low mortality rates, low prevalence of 
obesity, and minimal uninsured populations.

Depending on the scoring criteria of 
national assessments, North Dakota's 
general standing varies slightly:

Public Health Metrics (US News): 

North Dakota ranks among the top states for 
public health (frequently in the top 15–20 
nationwide), bolstered by high marks in 
low infant mortality, low smoking, and 
low suicide rates.Overall Health Care 
(America's Health Rankings): Evaluators 
generally place the state in the top 10 
nationwide for overall health and health 
system performance, reflecting high health 
status and low barriers to care.

Disaster Preparedness: Historically, the 
state ranks highly (frequently in the top 10)
for its emergency and public health emergency 
preparedness infrastructure.
