West Virginia's political economy is characterized by a 
historic transition from a union-strong, extraction-based 
economy to a predominantly conservative political 
landscape. The new regime that still relies heavily on the energy sector 
while struggling with low workforce participation, 
population decline, and persistently low median 
household incomes.

West Virginia is rich in actual and potential natural
resources, but its people are not. 

The severance tax on coal [and later natural gas] extraction -- the state's principal source of tradeable 
and taxable wealth -- was only introduced in 1987. 
Labor's historic role in the economy has been largely 
undermined by the state's unwillingness to invest in infrastructure, 
especially in its people. 

Senator Byrd's role on the Senate Appropriations 
Committee played no small part in slowing the rates of social decay 
associated worldwide with "the curse of natural resources"  and its West 
Virginia incarnation: "the coal dictatorship".

Education:

West Virginia funds public education through 
a combination of state income and local 
property taxes, with the state covering 
the majority of the operational costs -- including 
 employee compensation.

West Virginia competes annually for 
last place in US state educational rankings, highest 
poverty, and the lowest per capita income.
                                          
Energy Sector Dominance & Diversification  -- 
otherwise known as the *Curse of Natural Resources* 
all over the world.

The state’s economic fortunes remain tethered to 
extraction, with a long history of coal production 
now accompanied by significant natural gas output. 
While energy exports provide state revenue, West 
Virginia has struggled to foster a robust downstream 
manufacturing base, leaving the broader economy 
vulnerable to commodity price fluctuations and national 
shifts toward cleaner energy.

    
Healthcare:

West Virginia consistently ranks near the 
bottom nationwide for overall healthcare, 
generally placing between 46th and 50th across 
major national scorecards. While the state 
performs relatively well in health insurance 
coverage and preventative care visits, it suffers 
from poor health outcomes, high costs, and limited 
provider availability.
