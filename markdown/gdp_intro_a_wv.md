### Features of a Marxist approach to 'political economy'

#### Marx and the Classical Economists

While classical economists like Adam Smith
and David Ricardo defined political economy 
as the management of a nation's wealth and 
resources, Marx famously shifted the focus 
to a "Critique of Political Economy". 

He argued that traditional economics treated 
capitalist laws as eternal, natural 
conditions rather than temporary, 
historical systems built on the exploitation 
of the working class.Marx's understanding 
of political economy is built upon several 
core pillars.

Social Relations are more important in Marx than the 
material objects they interact with. Traditional 
economists viewed the economy as a simple 
interaction between things: for example, commodities,
prices, supply, and demand. 

Marx argued that economic categories are actually 
hidden social relationships between people. A 
commodity is not just an object; it is the 
physical embodiment of human labor-power 
that has been bought and sold in a market.

#### Production vs Consumption as a Starting Point

GDP (gross domestic product) is a good place to 
start in gaining a Marxist understanding of a state's 
_political economy_, or, its _"relations of 
production"_, as often referenced in Marx's writing.

The Marxist definition of _class_, for example, 
is entirely based on one's relationship to 
capitalist production, and is a key concept 
in understanding the dynamics – motion, production, and 
reproduction – of capitalist society.
